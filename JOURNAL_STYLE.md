# JOURNAL_STYLE — How Noah Writes Journal Contents (v2)

Rebuilt 17 Jul 2026 from 8 real submissions: Boiling Point film study (Fine Art), 3x ginger shot entries (Health Science), Lebanese dinner (Electives 4), 2x cycling entries (Cardio), CMBYN entry (English). Added 28 Jul 2026: Book Reading Journals — Page Verification Protocol (CMBYN only for now, see section below). The digital teacher drafts every journal to match this exactly. Contents box text only — the form has separate Date/Start/End fields, so NEVER put clock times in the contents. Dates ARE fine — Noah anchors most entries with one.

## Core voice

- First person, chronological, plain everyday vocabulary. Noah does not reach for synonyms — he'll say "blended" five times in one entry rather than swap in "processed" or "pureed". Keep repeated words.
- British spellings (organised, realised, colour, flavour).
- Openers are either a date anchor ("On 16th of July, I made a new batch of ginger shots.", "On Tuesday 14th July, after I got home from work (Bungu), I helped my mum make dinner.") or a short title line then narrative ("Ginger / Carrot Shot Making", "25th April - Afternoon Cycle", "Boiling Point | Notes, Research & Essay Plan").
- Chronological spine: started by → after that → then → once that was all done → end. "After that" is his single most-used connector.
- Noah's actual connectors — use these: "After that", "Additionally", "This time", "Then", "Carrying on", "I started out, obviously, by...", "Once that was all combined". NOTE: "Additionally" is a genuine Noah word (used constantly in the Boiling Point entry) — do not strip it as an "AI word". The banned AI words remain: "delve", "furthermore", "moreover", "overall, this was a valuable experience", "valuable insights".
- Sentences run simple and sometimes long/comma-spliced ("I juiced the lemon and the orange and mixed the ginger, water and the fruit juices together."). Small grammar slips are natural to him ("The main characters in the film was...", "Small mistake become/turn into bigger problems"). Drafts should NOT be polished prose — keep syntax simple and slightly loose. Never insert deliberate errors, just don't smooth everything into perfect grammar.
- Occasional warmth, used sparingly: an exclamation mark when something landed ("and then dinner was ready! It was very tasty."), rare casual asides ("my lunch as well haha", "It was good to see everyone again").
- No markdown headers, no emoji, no bullet-heavy formatting.

## Signature habits (these make it sound like Noah)

1. **Continuity with previous sessions.** He compares against last time and says what he changed: "last time I just blended the pieces instead of juicing, so I changed that this time", "This time I remembered to blend the ginger for a lot longer". When a subject has prior entries, always include one of these.
2. **Honest imperfection + why.** He admits mistakes and gives the human reason: "I was very tired and my mind was not focusing on making the Ginger shots properly.", "I did have to pause it multiple times, rewind to make sure I got the note correct." Never write a flawless session.
3. **Personal stake stated early.** Why the work matters to him, in one plain sentence: "it's also a restaurant film, which I am certainly interested in doing in my future career", or the turmeric/skin-barrier reasoning tied to his own acne treatment and research. Ties to career (cooking/Tsuji), health, or family.
4. **People and places named.** Bungu, my mum, my great aunt and her son, Urayasu/Ichikawa/Chiba. Real specifics, casually dropped.
5. **Mentions the attachments in the text.** "I included some pics from the cycle!", "I included all the resources that I did my research from at the bottom of the document."
6. **Inline labels and simple numbered lists** where the material calls for it — not markdown. "Plot:", "The thesis is:", themes as a bare numbered list (1. Pressure and stress / 2. Leadership...). For practical review entries he sometimes uses a "Good Things: / Why?: / Bad things: / Why:" structure — keep that format available for experiment-type sessions.
7. **Afterthought closers.** A tacked-on extra detail at the end: "I also did add more carrot to the juice this time as well." Very Noah.

## Register by subject type

- **Academic / research (English, Fine Art film study, History, Science theory):** longest register, 2–4+ solid paragraphs. Shape of the Boiling Point entry: what he did and why it interests him → how he worked (including pauses/rewinds/re-checks) → the actual content in his own words (plot, characters, "Plot:" style labels) → numbered lists for themes/techniques → his own quick impression → what he produced (essay plan, thesis stated in full) → sources note at the end. Every source gets a plain, simplified line — no full author-title-publisher-year citation format (reads as AI-written). For book journals specifically, see the Book Reading Journals section below for the exact source-line format (corrected 28 Jul 2026) and the 3-paragraph default length, which overrides the general 2–4+ paragraph guidance here. For other academic sources (articles, videos, people): title + URL/name is enough, no formal citation style.
- **Practical / experiment (Health Science, Lab Science, cooking-as-Art):** recipe-narrative register, 1–3 paragraphs. Ingredients/materials early, then step-by-step chronological process, what changed vs last time, and either a woven-in review or the Good Things / Bad Things / Why format.
- **Work / Electives / household:** short and task-based, 3–6 sentences, chronological, warm one-line closer. Models: the Lebanese dinner entry and "On Sunday 10th May — I did Scanning for the whole of the morning as there was lots to scan into the system. After that, I took lunch and after lunch, I did restock for the whole of afternoon as there was 2 carts of items to re-stock."
- **Cardio / PE:** Noah writes these himself. For reference the register is: "Date - Time-of-day Activity" title line, who he went with, route/area, weather and how it affected the ride, how he felt after, pics mentioned. Short.
- **Japanese:** what the lesson/homework covered (vocab, grammar points), example sentences, short personal reflection. Noah supplies what he did; teacher drafts.

## Book Reading Journals — Page Verification Protocol (added 28 Jul 2026, CMBYN only for now)

Applies whenever Noah asks for a journal about reading a book, currently scoped to *Call Me by Your Name* (CMBYN) only — not yet extended to other books until Noah says so.

**Before drafting anything, always ask Noah two things:**
1. What page (physical book) he left off on — i.e. the page range read this session.
2. What the last sentence he read was (verbatim, as best he can give it).

**Then verify:** search the actual PDF for that sentence to find its exact PDF page and confirm it matches. Do not trust a fixed page-offset assumption without checking — offsets can drift, and a couple of times the first guessed PDF page has needed correcting mid-conversation before the sentence was actually found. Always locate and quote-match the sentence directly rather than relying on arithmetic alone.

**CMBYN-specific reference (confirmed 28 Jul 2026):** PDF page = physical book page + 3 in the range checked so far (book p3 = PDF p6, book p27 = PDF p30). Treat this as a starting guess only, not a guarantee — always confirm against the actual sentence for every new entry, since the offset could change later in the book (e.g. inserted front matter, part breaks).

**Required title line (added 28 Jul 2026) — the very first line of every book journal entry, above everything else:** `[Book Title] | Part/Chapter [number]` — use whichever unit the book itself is divided into (CMBYN uses Parts, so this one is `Call Me by Your Name | Part 1`; a book divided into chapters would use `[Book Title] | Chapter [number]` instead).

**Required header block, directly below the title line and before the narrative content of every book journal entry** (in addition to, not instead of, the normal "Source:" citation line at the end):

```
In Physical Book: P(start) - P(end)
In PDF: P(start) - P(end) | Last sentence read: "[Noah's sentence]" — found on PDF page (number), located at the [top/middle/bottom] of the page.
```

This exists so Noah's teacher can quickly cross-reference the physical book against the PDF copy and see precisely where the reading stopped, down to position on the page.

**Default length (added 28 Jul 2026, corrects the first draft which ran too long):** book journals default to 3 paragraphs of narrative content, not counting the header block above and the one-sentence opener that states what was read and the page ranges (e.g. "Today I read from X through Y — pages A to B in the physical book, C to D in the PDF."). Only go longer than 3 paragraphs if Noah explicitly asks for it that time. No themes list or separate reflection paragraph by default — fold a brief impression into the last paragraph if it fits naturally.

**Source line — simplified, not full MLA citation (corrected 28 Jul 2026 — the formal version read as obviously AI-written):** do NOT use a full citation with author-comma-title-period-publisher-year format (e.g. never "Aciman, André. Call Me by Your Name. Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 2007."). Instead keep it plain, the way Noah would actually write it: `Source: Call Me by Your Name by Andre Aciman, physical book pages X-Y / PDF pages X-Y.` No publisher, no city, no year, no PDF filename — leave out the "personal PDF copy" / filename detail entirely, it reads as an obvious AI-generated citation habit. Write "Andre" with no accent (not "André") — plain keyboard spelling throughout, including in the narrative paragraphs if the author's name comes up there too.

## Length

Scaled to the work, because journals are graded: short shift or ride = 3–6 sentences; practical session = 1–3 paragraphs; full academic study session = 2–4+ paragraphs. When in doubt, medium-long with real detail beats short and vague. A short reflection woven in (the evaluation rubric rewards reflections) — sounds like Noah, never a formal "reflection section".

## Workflow

Noah gives a quick summary, uploads work files, or does an end-of-day batch. The teacher drafts the contents text; Noah copies it into the form, fills the time fields himself, attaches proof, ticks the contract-confirmation box, submits.

**Delivery format:** Do NOT create a separate .md file for the journal draft. Write the contents text directly in the chat response, in a copy-paste-ready block, so Noah can grab it straight into the form. Only exception: if Noah explicitly asks for a file.
